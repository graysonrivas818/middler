Cost to Paint a House in Pennsylvania (2026 Guide)
The cost to paint a house in Pennsylvania in 2026 typically ranges from $1,800 to $8,500, depending on whether you're painting the interior, exterior, or both, the home's size, and the condition of older plaster, trim, and siding. Smaller single-story homes can come in under $2,000, while larger Victorian and multi-story row homes in Philadelphia or Pittsburgh often push past $10,000 once prep and lead-safe work are factored in. What Factors Affect House Painting Costs in Pennsylvania?
Paint Quality
Contractor-grade latex runs $25–$45 per gallon; premium lines like Sherwin-Williams Emerald or Benjamin Moore Aura push $75–$90. Better paint lasts 7–12 years on PA exteriors vs 4–6 for economy lines.
Surface Condition
Peeling exterior paint, chalking, rotted wood trim, or damaged plaster walls can add 20–40% to the quote. PA's humid summers and hard winters accelerate wear on older wood siding.
Home Height & Layout
Three-story row homes in Philly, hillside homes in Pittsburgh, and tall Victorian gables in places like Wilkes-Barre or Bethlehem typically require lifts or scaffolding, which adds $300–$1,200 per project.
PA Labor Rates
Painters in Philadelphia charge $45–$85 per hour; Pittsburgh and Allentown run $40–$70; Harrisburg, Lancaster, Scranton, Erie, and Reading average $35–$60. Rural contractors often quote 15–25% less than big-city crews.
Prep Work
Scraping, sanding, caulking, priming, and minor repairs typically add 10–30% to the base cost. On homes older than 50 years — very common in PA — prep can sometimes cost more than the paint itself.
Lead Paint Remediation (Pre-1978 Homes)
Roughly two-thirds of PA homes were built before 1978, so lead testing is a real line item. A professional lead inspection usually costs $300–$700, and lead-safe prep work using EPA-certified RRP contractors adds $8–$17 per square foot of affected area. Full remediation on a whole house can run $10,000–$30,000.
PA Weather & Seasonality
Pennsylvania's humid summers and cold winters shorten the ideal exterior window. Spring bookings fill fast, late fall pricing creeps up as crews race the frost, and winter interior jobs often come with 10–20% off-season discounts.
Best Time of Year to Paint a House in Pennsylvania
The best months to paint the exterior of a Pennsylvania house are late April through early October, when temperatures hold between 50°F and 85°F and humidity stays manageable. May, June, and September tend to deliver the cleanest finishes — spring is dry enough to avoid surface moisture, and September pairs mild days with low pollen.
July and August still work but high humidity can slow paint cure, especially in Philadelphia and the Susquehanna Valley. Exterior painting is generally not recommended from mid-November through March due to freezing temps and low-temp paints having narrow application windows.
Interior painting is a year-round job, and winter is actually the cheapest time to book — many PA contractors offer 10–20% discounts from December through February to keep crews working.

Frequently Asked Questions About House Painting Costs in Pennsylvania
How much does it cost to paint a house in Pennsylvania? The cost to paint a house in Pennsylvania typically ranges from $1,800 to $8,500 in 2026. Interior-only jobs average $1,800–$5,500, exteriors run $2,500–$7,500, and full interior-plus-exterior projects land between $4,200 and $11,000.
How much does it cost to paint a house in Philadelphia vs Pittsburgh? Philadelphia averages $3.00–$7.50 per square foot, while Pittsburgh typically runs $2.50–$6.50 per square foot. A 2,000 sq ft interior job in Philly usually costs $5,500–$10,500; the same job in Pittsburgh is often $4,500–$9,000.
How much to paint a 2,000 sq ft house in PA? Expect $2,800–$6,200 for the interior and $3,800–$7,800 for the exterior of a 2,000 sq ft Pennsylvania home. Full interior-and-exterior projects typically run $6,500–$13,500.
Do I need a permit to paint the exterior of my house in PA? No state permit is required to paint a single-family home in Pennsylvania. However, homes in historic districts (parts of Philadelphia's Old City, Society Hill, Pittsburgh's Mexican War Streets, and Lancaster's historic core) often need color and material approval from a local historic commission before work begins.
How much extra does lead paint testing cost in Pennsylvania? A professional lead paint inspection in PA typically costs $300–$700. Full risk assessments with dust and soil sampling can reach $500–$900. If remediation is needed, expect $8–$17 per square foot of affected surface, or roughly $10,000–$30,000 for a whole house.
What's the cheapest month to paint a house in PA? January and February are usually the cheapest months to book an interior job in Pennsylvania, with many contractors offering 10–20% off-season discounts. For exteriors, late March and early November sometimes carry shoulder-season pricing — weather permitting.
Can I paint the exterior of my PA home in winter? Generally no. Most traditional exterior paints require surface and air temperatures above 50°F to cure properly. A few cold-weather formulas work down to 35°F, but PA winters also bring freeze-thaw cycles and moisture that compromise adhesion.
How much do painters charge per hour in Pennsylvania? Pennsylvania house painters typically charge $35–$85 per hour in 2026. Philadelphia crews are the most expensive at $45–$85, Pittsburgh and Lehigh Valley run $40–$70, and rural markets like Scranton, Erie, and Reading average $35–$60.
Does painting increase home value in Pennsylvania? Yes. A fresh interior paint job typically returns 1–3% of home value at resale, and a quality exterior repaint can recoup 50–100% of its cost. In competitive PA markets like the Philly suburbs, Pittsburgh's East End, and Lehigh Valley, fresh paint frequently helps homes sell faster and at higher prices.
