Interior vs Exterior Painting Costs in Ohio
Understanding the cost differences between interior and exterior painting helps you budget more accurately for an Ohio project.
Interior Painting
Interior painting in Ohio is usually cheaper because it involves:
Less setup and easier access
No weather delays
Faster drywall coverage on newer homes
Plaster repair in older homes can add 15-25% in labor
Average interior painting cost in Ohio: $1.75 - $3.50 per square foot
Exterior Painting
Exterior painting costs more in Ohio because it often includes:
Pressure washing to remove chalking and mildew
Scraping, priming, and caulking after winter damage
Ladders or scaffolding for colonials and multi-story homes
Extra masonry primer or specialty coatings for brick and cedar
Average exterior painting cost in Ohio: $2.25 - $4.25 per square foot

What Factors Affect House Painting Costs in Ohio?
Paint Quality - Contractor-grade paint runs $20-$40 per gallon. Mid-tier products like Sherwin-Williams SuperPaint and Behr Premium usually land at $45-$80. Premium lines like Duration, Aura, and Emerald can top $100 per gallon but last much longer on Ohio exteriors.
Siding Type - Vinyl is typically the cheapest surface to paint. Wood clapboard, cedar shake, and brick cost more because they need more prep, more primer, and often more paint.
Home Age and Prep Work - Ohio has one of the oldest housing stocks in the Midwest. Pre-1950 homes often add 10-30% because of scraping, caulking, plaster repair, and lead-safe practices.
Number of Stories - Each additional story raises labor materially. A three-story Cleveland colonial can cost close to double a single-story ranch with the same footprint.
Ohio Climate - Freeze-thaw cycles crack caulk and siding every winter. Quality primer and elastomeric products are often worth the added material spend on exterior jobs.
Labor Rates by Region - Ohio painters cost more in Columbus and Cleveland metros, where crew rates commonly run $45-$75 per hour, versus $35-$55 per hour in many rural counties.
Lead Paint Remediation - Homes built before 1978 require EPA RRP-certified contractors. Lead-safe work can add $8-$15 per square foot on impacted areas.

How to Estimate Your Ohio House Painting Cost in 3 Steps
You do not need a contractor visit to ballpark your number. This is the math painters use.
Measure your paintable square footage. For interiors, multiply perimeter by ceiling height, then subtract windows and doors. For exteriors, multiply wall height by width for each side.
Multiply by Ohio's average cost per square foot. Use $1.75-$3.50 for interior and $2.25-$4.25 for exterior.
Add 10-30% for prep, repairs, and premium paint. Older homes, brick, and multi-story builds lean toward the higher end.
Example: A 2,000 sq ft home in Columbus x $2.75 per sq ft = $5,500 for a base interior estimate. Add 15% for prep on a 1940s bungalow and you are at about $6,325.

Is It Cheaper to Paint Your Ohio Home Yourself?
DIY can save money, but only on the right project.
DIY pros
Save 60-75% on labor
Full control over paint choice and pace
Reasonable for single rooms, closets, and accent walls
DIY cons
Ohio's tall colonials and three-story Victorians are risky without proper scaffolding
Brick, stucco, and cedar require experience to prep correctly
Winter weather, humidity, and surprise rain can ruin a half-finished exterior
You will still spend $400-$900 on supplies, primer, and paint
Bottom line: DIY a bedroom or hallway. Hire a pro for most exteriors, any brick, and anything over one story.

When Is the Best Time to Paint a House in Ohio?
Ohio's exterior painting window is real, and good contractors schedule around it.
Best exterior window: late May through early October, when daytime temperatures usually stay between 60 F and 85 F and nights stay above 50 F.
Avoid: April if it is too wet, and November through March when paint cure becomes unreliable.
Winter interior discounts: Many Ohio painters offer 10-20% off interior work from December through February to keep crews busy.
